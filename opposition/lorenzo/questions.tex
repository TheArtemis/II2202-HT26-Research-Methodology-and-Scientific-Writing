\section*{1. Retained runs and expected cost}

\subsection*{Question statement}
\response{You calculate the average cost using only the 10 runs with the lowest token use out of 12. How well does this average represent what a developer would spend in practice, given that expensive runs can also happen?}

\subsection*{Motivation}
\response{Expensive runs may be real outcomes of using the agent, especially if one language is less predictable. Excluding them changes what the average represents and could affect the comparison between languages.}

\subsection*{Possible follow-up question}
\response{How would including all 12 runs, their variation, and any failed attempts affect your interpretation of the language ranking?}

\section*{2. Evidence for the compiler-feedback explanation}

\subsection*{Question statement}
\response{You suggest that Rust's compiler helps the model reach a working solution in fewer attempts. What evidence from your experiments supports this explanation?}

\subsection*{Motivation}
\response{The report observes a difference and proposes a cause, but lower cache-read totals could also reflect different amounts of context per call. Distinguishing direct measurements from hypotheses is necessary to assess whether compiler feedback explains the observed differences.}

\subsection*{Possible follow-up question}
\response{How could a follow-up experiment separate the effect of compiler feedback from other differences between the languages, such as the amount of code needed or how familiar the model is with each language?}
